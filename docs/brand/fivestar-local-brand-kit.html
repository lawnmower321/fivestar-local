Turn happy customers into 5-star Google reviews — with one tap. The visual system for everything FiveStar Local owns: the site's own chrome, cards and stands, social, and the internal console.

01 — Color Palette · “Cobalt & Honey”
Cobalt
#2749d6
Primary. CTAs, links, the tap-ripple, brand chrome. The trustworthy tech-blue — deeper and unmistakably not Google's azure.
Honey
#e8a317
Stars, highlights, focus rings. The color of a 5-star row. Use sparingly — it should always mean “review” or “attention.”
Ink
#0f1b3d
Headlines, dark sections, footers. A navy-cast near-black that keeps the page warm-cool rather than dead grey.
Slate
#5b6b83
Secondary text, captions, metadata. Continues the site's existing slate neutrals.
Cloud
#f7f9fc
Page and card backgrounds. White (#ffffff) sits on top of it for raised surfaces.

Google's palette (#4285f4 / #34a853 / #fbbc05 / #ea4335) appears only inside components that depict real Google UI — the review-showcase, scan-showcase, and tap-demo simulations. Never in FiveStar Local's own chrome, print, or packaging.

05 — Voice & Tone

Do

  • Speak plainly — “tap the card, leave a review.”
  • Lead with what happens, in order, in seconds.
  • Name real outcomes: more reviews, higher on the map, more walk-ins.
  • Be the people who show up — “we install it while you watch.”
  • Make promises concrete: fifteen seconds, before they're out the door.
  • Sound like a neighbor who happens to be good at this.

Don't

  • Use marketing superlatives — “revolutionary,” “game-changing.”
  • Reach for jargon — “omnichannel reputation management.”
  • Imply Google affiliation, in words or in color.
  • Overpromise rankings or guarantee outcomes.
  • Talk down to non-technical owners.
  • Hide the price or bury the ask.
